# Trade and token replay implementation

The server owns accounting and export eligibility. See [Node-owned financial data](../docs/node-owned-data.md) and [Durable evidence](../docs/node-evidence.md).

Public and authenticated Portfolio replay-preparation endpoints return one immutable accounting snapshot, its frozen ending valuation, an approved full-history window and genuine database market observations. Repeated polls join durable accounting work; browser preparation has one bounded poll loop and no legacy activity/chart retry chain. Closing a share modal cancels the subscriber and encoder without cancelling shared worker jobs.

Modern contracts use version 2 and truthful node, mixed or imported provenance. The exact ledger preserves FIFO costs, transfer receipt valuations and wallet-paid fees. Historical frame profit is realized gains plus the displayed remaining value minus remaining basis. ROI uses fee-inclusive acquisition capital. Unknown costs produce unavailable affected frames, rather than zero. Imported aggregate fee residuals apply only in the baseline/final state.

Long histories combine genuine coarse candles with a minute ending segment. Sparse coverage is disclosed, real timestamps stay intact, and a coarse interval cannot authorize a prolonged flat ending. Closed positions need historical market coverage, without a current quote. Open positions use the modal's frozen current mark. Token replays use the same market service and disclose estimated MCAP where applicable supply is uncertain.

The renderer keeps Kollens' line chart, branding, canvas dimensions, colors and deterministic seeking/export. Motion follows the supplied short and long reference videos: the newest price stays near the right edge, history initially pans left, and the chart then fits the full revealed history with its first point held near the left edge. Its price range expands for newly revealed extremes without repeatedly zooming in after pullbacks. Modern chart geometry uses real elapsed time independently of execution reading intervals, except for a dense opening in a long replay. Long replays with a dense opening keep the regular camera and give their first eight distinct movement groups a small, bounded 200ms reading allowance plus a fixed 3.5% share of horizontal story space. A monotone cubic mapping blends that space into the remaining chronological history; it never zooms back out or repositions old vertices. Axis labels retain actual timestamps on this paced, nonuniform timeline. The first-buy entrance remains 900ms and same-second groups remain atomic. Prices, actual timestamps and accounting are unchanged, and short or evenly spaced replays retain the regular camera. Each buy/sell amount appears centered above its execution at a constant font size, rises during a 200ms fade-in, briefly holds, then fades out by 420ms. Green plus/red minus signs are separate from the white dollar value. Overlapping amounts replace the older label in place; there are no arbitrary label columns, persistent trade tooltips, thesis overlays, big-sell highlights, badges, pulses, special zooms or slow motion. Version 2 movement groups sharing a timestamp are atomic; transfers have IN/OUT markers and preserve their movement identities. Compact estimate/final and provenance indicators accompany the result. A separately approved ending never replaces earlier unknown costs.

Wallet trade replays automatically use 8–25 seconds, based on the first recorded buy through final disposal or the frozen current valuation of an open position. Smooth logarithmic scaling gives a two-fill 2-minute position 9 seconds, an hour 14 seconds, a day 20 seconds and a week 25 seconds. Dense executions receive a bounded readability allowance; atomic same-second ledger groups count once. Modern ledger replays follow real elapsed time with a bounded minimum reading interval for dense execution groups, including post-trade market history. Chart padding and financial amounts do not affect duration. Closed results hold for 2 seconds and open ending marks for 1 second. Marker, amount-label and camera easing use playback seconds; the modal and exporter share the renderer's duration. Token-market replays retain their existing timing.

The paused share preview remains at the first buy throughout offscreen encoding and after export. The opening eases from rest over 900ms, with at least that much reading time before the next execution group. Earlier transfers and sales remain an accounting prefix, without preceding the opening buy visually. A sale-only history begins with its first available execution. Image sharing still captures the ending result offscreen while preserving the paused preview.

The pinned Mediabunny encoder produces every frame at 30 fps and 1080 × 1350 for the renderer's selected duration, always including its exact final state. AVC MP4 is preferred; supported WebM remains honestly labelled. MediaRecorder is only a pre-encoding capability fallback. Abort and encoding errors dispose partial output. Save image, copy image, video download and editable social share links keep their existing behavior; no automatic social posting occurs.

Regression coverage includes source identity, exact balance agreement, private membership revisions, frozen modal/video profit and ROI, moving historical profit, transfers, same-second groups, long charts, stale tails, coalescing, cancellation and actual local MP4 inspection.
